## 1. Foundational Cognitive Architecture & Decision Science

- **Dual-Process Model (System 1 / System 2)** — Fast, automatic, emotional, heuristic cognition (S1) vs. slow, deliberate, analytical, effortful cognition (S2); manipulation bypasses S2 and triggers S1.
- **Heuristic (Cognitive Shortcut)** — A mental shortcut for fast decisions without full analysis; one of the two universal manipulation targets.
- **Emotional Trigger** — A neurological response that bypasses rational deliberation; the second universal manipulation target.
- **Prospect Theory** — Humans are predictably irrational; decisions are reference-dependent and dominated by loss aversion (Kahneman & Tversky).
- **Limbic System Targeting** — The emotional brain receives input before the prefrontal cortex; techniques exploit this pre-rational sequencing.
- **The Map Is Not the Territory** — People respond to their internal perception of reality, not reality itself (NLP).
- **Unified Theory of Control** — All influence operates by controlling Information, Emotion, and/or Identity.
- **Influence Spectrum** — The continuum from legitimate persuasion → manipulation → coercion, distinguished by intent, transparency, and respect for autonomy.
- **Awareness Isn't Immunity** — Knowing a mechanism doesn't disable it; it only grants the choice whether to act on it.

## 2. Cialdini's Principles & Core Influence Levers

- **Reciprocity** — People feel compelled to return favors, gifts, or concessions, even unsolicited.
- **Commitment & Consistency** — Once committed (especially publicly), people stay consistent with the prior position.
- **Social Proof** — Under uncertainty, people use others' behavior as a cue to correct action.
- **Authority** — Compliance rises with perceived expertise/power (titles, uniforms, confident tone).
- **Liking** — People are more persuaded by those they like (similarity, compliments, familiarity, attractiveness).
- **Scarcity** — Opportunities seem more valuable when availability is limited.
- **Unity** — People are more influenced by those with whom they share identity/belonging (Cialdini's 7th, 2016).
- **BV's 12-Principle Synthesis** — Cialdini's 6+Unity extended with: Emotional Manipulation (FOG), Cognitive Dissonance Exploitation, Confirmation Bias Exploitation, Dunning-Kruger Exploitation, FOMO, Intermittent Reinforcement.

## 3. Persuasion Models & Routes

- **Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM)** — Two routes to attitude change (Petty & Cacioppo).
  - **Central Route** — Effortful, argument-based processing → durable, resistant attitude change.
  - **Peripheral Route** — Heuristic processing on superficial cues → shallow, reversible change.
- **Heuristic-Systematic Model (HSM)** — Systematic (analytical) vs. heuristic (shortcut) processing, governed by accuracy/defense/impression-management motives (Chaiken & Eagly).
- **Four-Factor Model of Persuasion** — Source, Message, Target, Situation (Hovland, Janis & Kelley 1953).
- **Fogg Behavior Model (B=MAP)** — Behavior occurs only when Motivation, Ability, and a Prompt converge simultaneously (B.J. Fogg).
- **Self-Perception Theory** — People infer their own attitudes from their own behavior (Bem) — basis of foot-in-the-door.
- **Cognitive Dissonance** — Discomfort from conflicting beliefs/behaviors, resolved by attitude change (Festinger).
- **Narrative Transportation** — Absorption in a story suspends critical evaluation (Green & Brock).
- **Neural Coupling** — Listener brain activity synchronizes with speaker's during a story (Hasson).

## 4. Cognitive Biases & Heuristics (exploitable levers)

- **Anchoring Effect** — The first numerical value disproportionately skews all later judgments.
- **Loss Aversion** — Losing hurts ~2× as much as an equivalent gain pleases (λ≈2.25).
- **Framing Effect** — Identical info framed as gain vs. loss yields different decisions ("90% survival" vs "10% mortality").
- **Confirmation Bias** — Seeking/interpreting/recalling info that confirms existing beliefs.
- **Availability Heuristic** — Overestimating the likelihood of vivid, recent, or emotionally charged events.
- **Sunk Cost Fallacy** — Continuing to invest because of prior investment rather than future value.
- **Halo Effect** — A positive impression in one domain (e.g. attractiveness) spills into unrelated competence/trust judgments.
- **Dunning-Kruger Effect** — Low-knowledge individuals overestimate their competence.
- **Bandwagon Effect** — Believing/doing things because many others do.
- **Status Quo Bias** — Preference for things to stay the same; resistance to change.
- **Default Effect** — Tendency to stick with the pre-selected option (organ-donor opt-in vs opt-out).
- **Mere Exposure Effect** — Repeated exposure increases liking absent any interaction.
- **Priming** — Prior stimuli silently activate related concepts and shift behavior.
- **Peak-End Rule** — Experiences are judged by their peak moment and their end, not the average (Kahneman).
- **Zeigarnik Effect** — Uncompleted tasks/open loops are remembered and preoccupy the mind.
- **Reactance Theory** — Threat to a freedom (scarcity) increases the desirability of the restricted thing (Brehm).
- **Affect Heuristic** — Emotion serves as a shortcut for judgment/decision-making.
- **Truth Bias** — Tendency to assume others are honest (Levine).
- **Illusory Truth Effect** — Repetition increases perceived truthfulness regardless of accuracy (Hasher et al. 1977).
- **Familiarity / Credibility Heuristic** — Familiar info judged truer; high-credibility sources inject misinformation more effectively.
- **Recency Bias** — Most recent statements carry disproportionate weight.
- **Subject-Validation Effect** — People accept vague statements as personally meaningful (cold reading).
- **Barnum Effect** — Statements that seem specific but apply to almost everyone.

## 12. Deception Detection (Behavioral Science)

- **Behavioral Baseline** — A person's truthful communication pattern; deviations (not isolated cues) are the signal.
- **Cognitive Load Principle** — Lying's added mental cost is the most reliable indicator.
- **Inducing Cognitive Load** — Reverse chronology · dual-task loading · unexpected/peripheral questions · drawing the scene.
- **Strategic Use of Evidence (SUE)** — Revealing evidence gradually/non-chronologically; confirms liars over-elaborate.
- **Verbal cues** — Inconsistent narratives (strongest) · anomalous detail level / bolstering · speech-rate & pitch shifts · disproportionate denial · excessive qualification · pronoun distancing.
- **Nonverbal cues** — Eye-contact anomalies · exit orientation · incongruent affect/gesture · blink-rate deviation.
- **Relational cues** — Sudden affective withdrawal · paradoxical increase in affection · information withholding below norm · heightened defensiveness.
- **Four-Step Field Protocol** — Calibrate with a known-true question → ask casually → apply cognitive load → verify longitudinal consistency.
- **Cluster Assessment (4+ rule)** — Require multiple simultaneous channel deviations; no single cue is diagnostic.
- **54% Accuracy Ceiling** — Human lie detection barely exceeds chance (Bond & DePaulo 2006).
- **Caveats** — Anxiety mimics deception · personal insecurity as confound · little lies don't predict big ones · motivated reasoning overrides accurate gut instinct.
- **Cognitive Interviewing** — Interview method surfacing accurate memory and inconsistencies.
