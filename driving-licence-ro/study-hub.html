Capitolul 1 · about 25 minutes

Drumul public: what the road is made of

The idea first

Before any rule makes sense, the law needs a shared vocabulary. When an exam question says acostament or bandă, it means something precise. This chapter is that vocabulary. It feels dry, but it's the easiest chapter to score full marks on, because it's pure recognition.

The single most important idea: the traffic code only applies on a public road (drumul public). So the law starts by saying exactly what counts as one.

Definiția · drum public orice cale de comunicație terestră, cu excepția căilor ferate, special amenajată pentru traficul pietonal sau rutier, deschisă circulației publice

Any land route, except railways, specially built for pedestrian or vehicle traffic and open to the public. Forest and farm tracks are usually not public roads. A road closed to the public must be marked at its entrance with the „Accesul interzis” sign plus a panel „Drum închis circulației publice”.

OUG 195/2002, art. 6 pct. 14 · Regulament, art. 3

The words you'll meet on the exam

You don't need to recite these. You need to recognise the correct one among three options. The highlighted bits are what the exam twists.

TermenWhat it meansWatch for
parte carosabilăThe part of the road platform meant for vehicles.A road can have several carriageways if they are completely separated.
acostamentThe side strip between the edge of the carriageway and the edge of the road platform.It is not part of the carriageway.

bandă de circulațieA lengthwise strip of the carriageway, marked or delimited, wide enough for one line of vehicles (not counting two-wheelers).Numbered from the right edge toward the centre line.

bandă de urgențăThe extra strip on the far right of a motorway.Not counted when numbering lanes. Not for driving or overtaking.

Capcane · the traps

Tram vs trolleybus. Both are electric public transport, but the tram runs on rails, so the law says it is not an autovehicul. The trolleybus is.

"Select all that apply" is always on. When two answers both look right, they often both are. Read all three before ticking.

Absolute words. Options with întotdeauna (always), numai (only) or oricând (anytime) are wrong more often than not. Example: the centre line does not always sit in the middle.

Railways are excluded. A railway is a land route too, which is exactly why the definition of drum public has to exclude it by name.

Notițe · capitolul 1 in six lines
  • The traffic code applies on the drum public: any land route except railways, built for pedestrians or vehicles, open to the public.
  • Outside towns: șanț · acostament · parte carosabilă · acostament · șanț. Inside towns: trotuar · rigolă · parte carosabilă · rigolă · trotuar.
  • Lanes are numbered from the right edge toward the centre line. The motorway emergency lane isn't counted.
  • Vehicul includes bicycles. Autovehicul excludes trams and farm/forestry tractors, but includes trolleybuses.
  • A localitate only exists legally where its entries and exits are signposted.
  • Rampă = rising, palier = flat, pantă = falling, always judged from your direction of travel.
