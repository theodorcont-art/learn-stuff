# Core Points

#### 4. Overcoming Power Games: Identifying and Countering Covert Power Moves  
**Power moves** are any actions affecting the distribution of power in social encounters. A distinctive feature is that many are **covert**—they appear neutral or polite but subtly disempower you to benefit the mover.  

Common covert power moves include:  
- **“I was busy”:** Implies you’re a low priority. Best handled by indifferent but polite acknowledgment or ignoring the subtext.  
- **“I’m sorry” (non-apology apologies):** Put responsibility back on you (e.g., “Sorry you felt hurt”). Neutralize by promptly moving the conversation on or (if appropriate) asserting forgiveness to regain power.  
- **“Sorry, I don’t remember you”:** Denies your value. Counter by feigning forgetfulness or lightly demeaning them to equalize the power balance.  
- **“You can do it!” (cheap encouragement):** Implies weakness and neediness. Resist affirming their frame and use humor or minimal acknowledgment to deflect.  
- **“How can I help you?”:** Puts you in the “taker” position. Reframe to “perhaps we can help each other” to assert reciprocal value.  

Additional nuanced dynamics include **judge power moves**, where the mover passes judgment to assert superiority (e.g., “That’s why I like you” as patronizing approval). Respond by mirroring the frame humorously or flipping the judgment back.  

**Microaggressions** are low-intensity, often joking insults used as subtle power plays. Effective responses include:  
- **Surfacing:** Probing the intent behind the “joke.”  
- **Vulnerability:** Expressing how they make you feel to shame the aggressor.  
- **Positive reframing:** Turning the insult into a strength or principle.

#### 5. Winning Negotiations via Advanced Frame Control  
**Frame control** is the art of influencing how others perceive a situation—shaping their **lens** to see you as high-value and align interests for mutual gain.

- **Refusing frames:** Directly or indirectly reject others’ imposed frames, especially when inappropriate.  
- **Frame buffet:** Selectively engage with parts of a frame to diffuse conflict or control the conversation.  
- **Reframing meaning:** Change the interpretation of a comment or situation to positive or neutral.  
- **Philosopher’s frame:** Use broad, abstract truths to deflect attacks and avoid commitment to contentious positions.  
- **Win-win frames:** Emphasize mutual benefit in negotiations, seek agreements that uplift both parties.  

**Practical application examples:**
